How to choose a sunscreen: check the amount before the SPF

{{ readMinutes }} min read

Looking clean and put-together shows up not only in your clothes and hair but in your skin. And one of the things that shapes how your skin looks is the UV light you take in, a little at a time, every day.

{% quiz "True or false: put on an SPF 50 sunscreen and you get SPF 50 protection.", "False. It depends on how much you apply.", "SPF is measured with 2 mg of sunscreen per square centimeter of skin. People usually apply far less than that, and when the amount drops, protection drops sharply." %}

When choosing a sunscreen, it's tempting to compare the numbers. But the first question is whether you'll actually apply it in a sufficient amount, every day.

People who applied it daily showed less skin aging

A randomized controlled trial in Australia split 903 adults under 55 into a group that applied sunscreen every day and a group left to use it at their own discretion, then compared the skin on the backs of their hands four and a half years later [1].

The daily group showed no detectable increase in skin aging over those four and a half years, and their skin aged 24% less than the discretionary group's [1].

POINT

What mattered wasn't "a strong sunscreen, sometimes," but "applying it every day."

What SPF and PA mean

Sunscreen labels in Japan carry two ratings [2]. (Outside Asia you'll often see "broad spectrum" or a UVA logo instead of PA.)

LabelProtects mainly againstHow to read it
SPFUVB (the cause of sunburn)Higher numbers mean more protection (up to "50+" in Japan)
PAUVA (reaches deeper into the skin)More "+" signs mean more protection (PA+ to PA++++)

If visible aging is what you care about, check the UVA rating as well as the SPF.

What's usually missing is the amount

SPF is measured with 2 mg of sunscreen per square centimeter of skin. But when a study asked beachgoers to apply sunscreen themselves, they used on average about 0.5 mg per square centimeter, roughly a quarter of the test amount [3].

A study of the relationship between amount and SPF found that protection falls off faster than the amount does [4]. A thin layer of SPF 50 is not SPF 50.

Japan's Ministry of the Environment manual suggests, for the face, taking a pearl-sized amount of cream (or a one-yen-coin-sized pool of liquid), dotting it on your forehead, nose, both cheeks, and chin, spreading it out, and then applying the same amount again. Because sweat and time wear it off, it also recommends reapplying every two to three hours [2].

Guidelines for choosing

The Ministry of the Environment manual gives these rough guidelines by activity [2].

ActivitySuggested SPF / PA
A short walk or shoppingSPF 10–20, PA+ to ++
Short outdoor play or light exerciseSPF 20–40, PA++ to +++
Long hours outdoors, at the beach or in the mountainsSPF 30–50, PA++ to ++++

Beyond that, when I pick something for everyday use, I look at three things.

  • Is it pleasant enough to put on every day? (Greasiness, white cast, smell.) If you don't keep using it, it does nothing
  • Does it have a UVA rating?
  • On days you'll sweat, is it sweat- and water-resistant?

If it doesn't agree with your skin (redness, itching), stop using it. If your skin is sensitive, try a small amount on the inside of your arm before using it on your face.
