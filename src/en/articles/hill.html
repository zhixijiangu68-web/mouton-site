Is "Think and Grow Rich"
actually true?

{{ readMinutes }} min read

Napoleon Hill's Think and Grow Rich. (Its Japanese title translates as "Thoughts Become Reality.")

In the world of self-help, it's about as famous as a book gets.

To be honest, going by the title alone, I was suspicious too.

Think it, and it happens.

Wish hard enough, and it becomes real.

If that's all it said, it wouldn't be worth reading.

But reading it changed my impression.

What it describes isn't magic. It's fairly plain, practical stuff.

You can't chase what you haven't thought about

There's a person who thinks, "Someday I want to do something,"

and a person who thinks, "Within three years, I'll build my own service and earn enough from it to live on."

Both have a dream.

But what catches their eye day to day is different.

The second person starts looking: what to make, who it's for, what skills it takes, who they could learn from.

The world didn't change.

The person looking at it did.

What Hill describes is "how to turn a desire into action"

As steps for turning desire into reality, Hill lists things like these:

Decide exactly what you want, not vaguely.

Decide what you'll give in return for it.

Set a deadline: by when.

Make a plan and start moving, even if you aren't ready.

Write it down.

Read it aloud every day.

It isn't "wish and it comes true."

It's "break your desire down until it's something you can act on."

If your destination stays vague, you don't know which way to go.

Only once you decide "I'm going there" can you choose a road.

I think desire isn't a spell. It's what gives a life direction.

Thoughts don't change reality directly

This part is important.

Thinking every day that you want a million yen won't raise your bank balance.

Imagining your ideal body won't build muscle.

Just wanting to write a book won't put a single word on the page.

Thought → judgment → action → persistence → results

Thought sits at the very start of that chain.

And when the start changes, the choices after it change.

Get up, or sleep a little longer. Read a book, or scroll social media. Make something, or put it off.

Each choice is small.

But stacked over one year, three years, ten years, they make a big difference.

Maybe what becomes real is "repeated thought"

Something you think once disappears quickly.

But what you think about again and again stays.

You think about it in the morning. On the train. Before bed.

Then you look things up. Talk to people. Try. Fail. Think again.

Before long, that thought becomes part of your daily life.

When daily life changes, reality slowly changes too.

Maybe that's why Hill wrote "read it every day."

The book doesn't say this. It's my own interpretation.

But not everything is decided by thought

The circumstances you're born into. Money. Illness. Accidents. The economy. Other people's decisions. And chance.

Life has things you can't do anything about.

So I don't think it's right to say simply: they succeeded because their thinking was right; they failed because they didn't think enough.

Effort doesn't guarantee success.

Wishing hard doesn't guarantee it comes true.

Even so, within the part you can change, thought plays a large role.

That has value.

This book hands you a question, not an answer

What stayed with me after reading wasn't a feeling of "I'll believe."

It was the realization: "I'm still vague about what I actually want."

So I think this book suits people whose goals are a bit blurry, people who think a lot but can't keep acting, and people who find self-help a little dodgy.

The last group, especially, should read it.

You can read it with doubt.

You don't have to swallow it whole.

You don't have to believe all of it.

Find, in your own words, "this part I can use."

I think that's the best way to read this book.

So what does "think and grow rich" mean?

I don't take the title to mean "think it, and the universe will grant your wish."

You think. A direction is set. Your choices change. Your actions change. You repeat it.

As a result, reality changes, little by little.

Seen that way, the idea that thoughts become reality suddenly looks very practical.

Let me leave you with one question.

What you think about almost every day: what future is it leading to?

If that thinking is building who you'll be a year from now,

what will you think about today, and what will you start?

Some hints are in the book.
